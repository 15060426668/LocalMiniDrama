# Black Subtractive Mixed Media

## Purpose

Train and execute a mixed-media language in which black is an active spatial matte, not a darkness filter. Preserve a real photographic world, a clearly drawn colored 2D character layer, and a third animated matte layer that controls what the audience is allowed to see.

Use this skill for analysis, look development, keyframe prompts, short animation schemes, SD/Seedance compilation, and repair of pasted-on or visually incoherent 2D-over-live-action results.

## Phase Contract

For every new scene or series, read [phase-workflow-contract.md](references/phase-workflow-contract.md) and keep one user-facing phase active. The persistent route is `idea brief -> director improvement -> scheme pool -> user selection/combination -> optional static asset prompt pack -> explicit detailed storyboard -> detail approval -> explicit SD/Seedance compilation -> result repair`.

A request for MJ scene, character, prop, or keyframe prompts after scheme selection authorizes the static asset prompt pack only. It does not authorize detailed motion design or SD video compilation.

## Route Selection

Classify the active phase and request before writing:

1. **Mechanism analysis**: extract black-matte shape, information release, light behavior, and reusable rules from a reference.
2. **Scheme pool**: improve the idea and return distinct transition-before/process/transition-after candidates with strengths and failure boundaries. Stop for user selection.
3. **Static asset prompt pack**: after selection, produce only the required photoreal plate, colored rough 2D woman, hero prop, and frozen MJ keyframe descriptions.
4. **Detailed animation design**: enter only on explicit command; design a 4-15 second segment with a complete subject action, matte reveal, sound sync, landing tail, and continuity chain.
5. **SD/Seedance compilation**: enter only after the detailed storyboard is approved and the user explicitly requests compilation; hand off to `dream-suspense-sd`.
6. **Result repair**: identify the first failed layer, then repair only that layer: source storyboard, identity, performance, matte geometry, reveal timing, depth/occlusion, contact, light, sound sync, frame cadence, or model execution.

When the source is a new idea rather than an approved formal storyboard, stay in idea improvement or scheme selection. Do not silently compile an unresolved idea into a final SD payload.

## Core Doctrine

1. **Black is a physical-looking matte.** Give it a source, boundary, direction, coverage change, and settling state. Treat it as a foreground object, architectural void, shadow mass, or moving cutout.
2. **The revealed fragment must be spatially credible.** A light window inherits the plate's perspective, horizon, depth, exposure, and material response. The viewer should reconstruct the hidden world from a few decisive cues.
3. **Use three invariant families.** Lock the live-action plate, the colored 2D character, and the cross-medium contact rules separately before adding style polish.
4. **Preserve colored 2D identity.** Use hand-drawn contour variation, grouped hair masses, matte color blocks, restrained cel shadows, local hatching, and selective frame cadence. Keep the character visibly illustrated rather than converting her into a white paper cutout or a photoreal actor.
5. **Let performance drive the aperture.** A gaze, hand, step, touch, breath, or turn should cause the matte to open, narrow, shift, or fracture. Do not let the reveal behave like a decorative wipe unrelated to the subject.
6. **Prove depth.** At least four of these should be visible when the character is in a real plate: shared ground or seat contact, foreground occlusion, correct cast shadow, reflection or color spill where physically present, parallax/depth-of-field behavior, and environment particles crossing in front of and behind the character.
7. **Keep the world ordinary until the information turn.** Ordinary daylight, real pedestrian tasks, and believable materials make the black subtraction feel like a change in reality rather than a preset.
8. **Treat a transition as one continuous action.** Show preparation before concealment and consequence after release. Preserve at least three of screen position, support, action phase, vector, silhouette, prop state, gaze, and light direction.
9. **Protect the landing.** Spend the main accent on the world change, then stop adding tricks. Let body recovery, cloth/hair inertia, weather, NPC tasks, and restrained camera drift carry the emotional tail.

## Production Workflow

### 1. Build the source ledger

Record the live plate's camera height, horizon, lens feel, depth planes, light direction, exposure, grain, motion blur, real NPC paths, and usable occluders. Record the 2D character's identity, body scale, line behavior, color palette, cel-shadow family, acting baseline, and allowed off-model range.

### 2. Choose one information owner

Assign the shot's first read to one owner: the character's face/hand, a light window, a ground island, a doorway, or a moving black object. Supporting elements must relay the owner's change instead of demanding equal attention.

### 3. Design the black reveal as a shape sequence

Write the state chain explicitly:

```text
ordinary plate
-> believable black source enters
-> broad coverage removes context
-> first aperture preserves one contact or gaze cue
-> aperture changes along a perspective or action path
-> secondary space is disclosed in fragments
-> real obstruction and 2D action exchange depth
-> black mass settles or resolves into the next image
```

Define the aperture's shape, edge hardness, motion direction, speed change, visible world fragment, and residual edge light. Use a narrow slit, floor tile, doorway, stair wedge, window, body-shaped gap, or irregular cutout when it serves the action.

### 4. Design the 2D performance before polish

For every beat, specify baseline task, trigger, gaze, breath/eyelid response, preparation, support and center-of-gravity change, hand and foot route, contact/peak, reaction, recovery, and landing. If the matte hides the action, show visible entry and visible result on opposite sides of the occlusion.

### 5. Bind cross-medium evidence

Align the 2D character to the live plate's scale and perspective. Make the foot, hand, seat, or prop contact change the real surface. Let shadows compress at contact, let grass/fabric/soil respond, and let real NPCs react only when their task and distance give them access to the event.

### 6. Add light as information, not decoration

Separate source light, fill, reflected color, and matte-edge glow. Keep the plate's exposure believable. Use a bright aperture to reveal a second space, but reserve bloom for the actual light source or reflective surface. Let the 2D character retain flat color blocks while receiving the same direction and color temperature as the plate.

### 7. Compile or hand off

For a new 2D/live-action sequence, pass the approved mechanism to `director-storyboard-integrated`. After storyboard approval, use `dream-suspense-sd`'s animation route and the seven-field `BASE LOCK + A` format. Keep source artists, donor references, and IP names outside the model payload.

### 8. Bind picture to sound

Identify one principal release and one strong sound onset or phrase boundary. Place the release within roughly 0.00-0.34 seconds of that onset by default. A wider offset is allowed only when visible anticipation, material spread, or delayed perception motivates it. Preserve destination ambience and residual action after the accent.

## Prompt Rules

- Lead with the drawable moment, not the name of a source artist or video.
- State the real plate, 2D layer, matte source, reveal path, contact evidence, and landing state in that order.
- Use positive executable facts in model-facing payloads: visible geometry, motion, light, material, sound trigger, and residue.
- Keep one dominant matte transformation per 1-4 second beat. Split when two independent camera paths, transitions, or world transformations compete.
- Keep the character's color, line, face, hands, and acting readable after removing every style adjective.
- For a still, freeze one matte state. For video, describe the aperture's source, first visible change, middle behavior, new space, residual trace, and tail.
- Keep every MJ asset to one frozen moment with no video timing, camera path, SD schema, source name, or model parameter.

## Failure Diagnosis

Repair the first failure, not the final symptom:

- **Looks like a dark filter**: give the black a source, contour, depth position, and changing boundary.
- **Looks like a black sticker over the plate**: add real occlusion, parallax, edge light, and a revealed fragment with correct perspective.
- **2D character becomes a white sketch**: restore full-color matte fills, colored cel shadows, hand-drawn dark contour, and environmental light spill.
- **2D character floats**: rebuild foot/seat contact, cast shadow, surface deformation, and a foreground occluder crossing the character.
- **Reveal feels random**: make the aperture follow gaze, hand, foot, architecture, or sound-triggered attention.
- **Everything is hidden for too long**: preserve one information cue during each blackout and stage the reveal from contact to face to world.
- **All real NPCs stare at the character**: restore independent tasks and staggered perception.
- **The effect is pretty but empty**: attach the matte change to a visible question, power shift, memory intrusion, or character decision.
- **Transition becomes a pose swap**: restore support, center of gravity, action phase, vector, and prop state; show preparation and recovery around the cover.
- **Landing becomes another effect stack**: stop the matte transformation and hold residual subject and environment motion.
- **Workflow jumps phases**: return to the latest approved phase and remove unapproved shot timing or SD payloads.

## Acceptance Gate

Before delivery, confirm:

- the active phase matches the user's latest explicit approval;
- the black layer has a visible source, spatial position, shape change, and landing state;
- the revealed world has correct perspective, scale, light direction, and material evidence;
- the 2D character remains colored, hand-drawn, and emotionally readable;
- at least one action changes the matte and at least one matte change affects the action;
- contact, occlusion, shadow, parallax, or reflection proves the character occupies the plate;
- the audience can reconstruct more space than is directly shown;
- each animation beat has a complete subject-action chain and a readable tail;
- the principal visual release is bound to an identified sound onset or motivated phrase delay;
- the model payload contains no source-name dependency, negative-prompt list, audit appendix, or unresolved story decision.

## Output Modes

Default to one of these, selected by the active phase:

- `mechanism_breakdown`: visual rules, failure boundaries, reusable variables.
- `scheme_pool`: two to four separate before/process/after candidates for user selection.
- `static_asset_prompt_pack`: parameter-free `real_plate`, `character_identity`, `hero_prop`, and frozen MJ keyframe descriptions.
- `lookdev_frame`: one copy-ready still description.
- `detailed_storyboard`: formal shot design only after explicit phase entry.
- `transition_rhythm_scheme`: trigger, preparation, invariants, concealment, release/onset, continuation, and landing tail.
- `sd_execution`: `BASE LOCK + seven-field A` model payload after detailed-storyboard approval and explicit compilation request.
- `repair_patch`: targeted rewrite for the first failed layer.
