# Black Mask Grammar

## Visual Thesis

Black subtraction is an information architecture. It removes context while preserving a few high-value cues: a foot on a surface, a hand crossing a light edge, a face in a slit, a road vanishing toward a bright opening, or a real object continuing behind an invisible wall.

The target effect is not darkness. It is a controlled exchange between:

```text
visible ordinary world
-> black coverage
-> contact cue
-> spatial aperture
-> partial world reconstruction
-> altered attention or reality
```

## Operating Modes

Choose one mode before designing boundaries.

### Spatial matte reveal

Black changes during a shot and behaves as a foreground obstruction, architectural void, moving cutout, or controlled aperture. Give it a source or a readable entrance, a depth position, a direction, a coverage path, and a landing state.

### Editorial dead-black reconstruction

Black permanently deletes connective context from a photographic plate. It is an editing decision, not an object in the depicted world. It does not require a diegetic source, but every surviving fragment must retain coherent lens perspective, scale, exposure, and internal motion. Compose the remaining fragments as support island, path, distant window, relationship fragment, and scale anchor.

Do not force a portal, curtain, shadow, or doorway explanation onto an editorial reconstruction. Do not use editorial dead black as an excuse for random collage.

## Boundary Families

Choose a boundary that belongs to the scene:

- **Architectural**: doorframe, corridor, window, stairwell, roof gap.
- **Ground-based**: pavement strip, floor tile island, shoreline, grass patch, platform edge.
- **Body-based**: silhouette gap, sleeve opening, hair curtain, shoulder turn, hand-shaped cutout.
- **Object-based**: passing coat, vehicle, umbrella, signboard, tree trunk, foreground prop.
- **Light-based**: beam, reflected rectangle, screen glow, skylight, water shimmer.
- **Geometric**: a sharp rectangle, narrow slit, angular wedge, broken grid, or irregular island when the design is deliberately graphic.

The boundary must carry a direction or weight. A passive centered vignette usually reads as grading rather than subtraction.

For editorial reconstruction, add these composition families:

- **Support island**: retain only the surface directly carrying a person or object.
- **Perspective path**: retain a road, stair, pavement, or platform wedge that links a small figure to camera.
- **Remote world window**: retain one sky, canopy, water, or room rectangle separated from the subject.
- **Split-depth pair**: retain one near architectural wedge and one far photographic window from the same plate.
- **Micro-window constellation**: scatter a small number of distant context fragments above or around a grounded figure.
- **Monument ladder**: reveal object, person, support, and world in a scale-changing sequence.

## Reveal Ladder

Reveal information in an intentional order:

1. **Contact**: feet, seat, hand, object edge, grass bend, or cast shadow.
2. **Intent**: gaze, breath, eyelid, hand route, or body lean.
3. **Identity**: face, hair mass, clothing color, or prop ownership.
4. **World**: architecture, road, tree line, crowd task, weather, or horizon.
5. **Consequence**: the revealed space changes behavior, sound, light, or the matte boundary.

Skipping directly from total black to a full establishing shot usually loses the psychological charge. Use a small contact cue before the world opens.

## Black Coverage Rhythm

Treat coverage as a rhythm rather than a constant opacity:

```text
wide ordinary context
-> fast occlusion
-> near-total black hold with one readable cue
-> narrow aperture
-> aperture growth along a subject or perspective path
-> partial overlap of old and new spaces
-> resolve, recoil, or hard cut
```

The hold gives the audience time to imagine the hidden world. The reveal must then be caused by a clear visual or audible trigger.

Apply this rhythm only to spatial matte animation. For a reconstructed still, freeze a complete fragment hierarchy instead: support first in visual importance, subject second, path or task object third, remote world last.

## Light Rules

- Let the revealed space retain its own white balance and material color.
- Keep black edges dense and mostly neutral; give them a narrow motivated rim only when a real source supports it.
- Use bloom on a lamp, sunlit opening, glass, water, or reflective metal, not on every edge.
- Let 2D characters receive the plate's source direction while keeping their own flat color and line hierarchy.
- Use a small color bridge between worlds: grass green on a lower sleeve, warm doorway light on a cheek, blue window fill in hair, or reflected road color on a shoe.
- In editorial mode, let glow exist only around retained pixels that are genuinely bright. Neutral pavement, clothing, walls, or grass should not acquire a generic neon halo.

## Mechanism Variants

### Doorway disclosure

Black behaves as a closed room. A slit opens on the character's gaze or hand contact, revealing a real location behind the expected space.

### Ground-island displacement

Only the surface that supports the character remains lit. The island moves, fractures, or extends into a path while the surrounding world stays black.

### Passing-body occlusion

A real person or object crosses close to lens, turning a physical pass into a black field. When it exits, the world returns with a changed aperture and a different information owner.

### Light-carved body

The black mass follows a 2D character's silhouette, leaving only face, hand, hair edge, or foot visible. The character's action controls which body part becomes available.

### Spatial collage

Several fragments of one real scene appear as disconnected tiles or windows. Keep each fragment internally perspectival and align their light direction so the discontinuity feels intentional.

## Common Misreads

| Misread | Repair language |
|---|---|
| Dark cinematic grade | black matte has a physical edge, a depth position, and a changing coverage path |
| Random portal | aperture follows gaze, hand, foot, architecture, sound, or a visible force |
| White paper cutout | colored 2D character with matte fills, colored cel shadows, dark hand-drawn contour, and plate-colored light |
| Empty void | preserve contact, scale cue, perspective fragment, and one human task |
| Glowing outline effect | edge light comes from an actual opening or reflective surface and falls off into the plate |
| Full-screen VFX takeover | black remains subordinate to the subject's question and returns a readable tail |
| Generic glitch preset | use missing context, crop displacement, fragment repetition, and temporal dropping; omit RGB split, CRT scanlines, pixel sorting, and datamosh unless directly observed |
| Glossy 3D void | restore ordinary photographic compression, imperfect weather, unstyled materials, and live human micro-motion |
| Neon portal | remove uniform rim light; keep only source-motivated falloff around bright retained surfaces |
| Floating cutout person | preserve a support island, contact shadow, task object, and movement inside the retained real surface |
