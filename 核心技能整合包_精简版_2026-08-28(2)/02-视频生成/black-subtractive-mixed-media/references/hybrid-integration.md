# 2D Character In Live-Action Plate

## Three-Layer Lock

Write three separate invariant blocks before any timed action.

### Live-action plate

Lock framing, camera height, horizon, lens behavior, depth planes, light direction, exposure, grain, motion blur, real NPC routes, and usable foreground occluders.

### Colored 2D character

Lock identity, body scale, silhouette, face/eye grammar, hair masses, wardrobe colors, line pressure, fill boundaries, cel-shadow family, ordinary acting baseline, and allowed line boil or stepped exposure.

### Cross-medium support

Lock foot/seat/hand contact, cast-shadow direction and softness, foreground/background occlusion, reflection or color spill where present, depth-of-field relationship, real prop ownership, and particle layers crossing the character.

When the subject is distant, lock silhouette, posture, prop weight, and support contact before face details. Do not spend prompt budget on irises or cheek hatching that cannot be resolved at the chosen scale.

## Spatial Evidence

Use at least four evidence types in a normal hybrid shot, and all three contact/occlusion/light evidence types when the 2D character touches the real world:

1. Shared ground or seat: body weight compresses grass, cloth, soil, pavement, or furniture.
2. Occlusion exchange: a real object crosses in front of the 2D character, while the character covers a slice of the background.
3. Correct projection: cast shadow follows the plate source and changes with support or separation.
4. Reflection or transmission: glass, water, metal, or bright floor carries a restrained 2D trace when physically present.
5. Parallax and focus: the character holds the plate's depth plane while retaining a visible drawn contour and stepped exposure.
6. Environment crossing: dust, rain, leaves, steam, or tree shadow exists in both front and back layers and leaves a visible consequence.

For editorial dead-black reconstruction, keep contact, shadow, and reflection inside the visible photographic support fragment. Do not make a physical shadow travel into the black editorial field. Use a second fragment, a crossing particle, or continued motion to prove shared reality instead.

## Animation Cadence

Keep the plate's camera motion and real NPC movement continuous. Let the 2D character use selective exposure:

```text
moving hold for breath and gaze
-> denser key poses at action peak
-> short line boil or offset contour near force
-> body settles first
-> hair, cloth, particles, line boil, and contact shadow settle after
```

Do not make every part jitter at the same rate. Keep eyes, nose, mouth, hands, and contact points readable.

For deliberately rough colored linework, keep contour instability local: doubled sleeve edges at force, a brief hair-line offset during a turn, or incomplete contour closure around cloth. Keep eyes, hands, luggage handles, feet, and support points stable enough to read.

## Contact Chain

Compile contact as an observable sequence:

```text
look at target
-> approach along the plate perspective
-> prepare and decelerate
-> 2D contour overlaps the real surface
-> contact shadow tightens
-> real surface or object responds
-> 2D body receives resistance and weight
-> nearby NPC reacts only after perceiving the event
-> release changes shadow, crease, reflection, or object position
-> tail preserves the residue
```

## Repair Order

Repair in this order:

1. depth position and occlusion;
2. ground/contact and cast shadow;
3. shared light direction and color spill;
4. scale, perspective, focus and motion blur;
5. line, fill, cel shadow and stepped exposure;
6. local atmosphere and finish texture.

Polishing line texture before fixing contact makes the character look more pasted on.

## User-Specific Character Guardrails

- Preserve a fully colored 2D figure with dark blue-gray variable contour, low-saturation matte fills, restrained cel shadows, broad grouped hair masses, and limited local hatching.
- Do not drift into pure black-and-white manga, a white paper cutout, generic clean anime, or photoreal skin.
- Preserve the requested subject count and gender. Do not invent boys, men, male friends, or male protagonists when none were requested.
