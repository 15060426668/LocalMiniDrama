## Source A: Digital Solitude And Fragmented Continuity

### Timeline observations

- **00:00-00:08, park walkers:** begin with a person-sized vertical crop, add a second crop, a bench strip, a bin block, then a large tree canopy. The fragments remain ordinary daylight photography; black removes the connective tissue.
- **00:12-00:17, umbrella walker:** a narrow upright doorway shows the body first. A lit pavement wedge grows toward camera and converts a small figure into a long-distance event.
- **00:19-00:26, cyclist:** preserve a tiny street slit, then extend only the road markings into a tapering perspective path. Motion is readable from displacement inside the path, not from showing the city.
- **00:27-00:42, escalator:** keep the concrete wall and escalator as a lower architectural wedge, then add a warm upper rectangle from the same station. The two fragments create a stage-like depth relation without revealing the whole station.
- **00:43-00:52, seated pair:** retain a thin grass support island, then scatter miniature vertical park fragments above the figures. A large pale sky window opens last. The sequence moves from support, to human relation, to inaccessible world.
- **00:53-00:68, monument:** isolate the pedestal first, add a tiny person on a separate tile, reveal the sky/trees as a large rear rectangle, and finally join the foreground base. Scale changes from object, to monument, to place.

### Expression mechanism

The person is often too small for facial acting. Emotion comes from occupancy and missing context:

```text
ordinary task
-> support fragment survives
-> most social context is removed
-> movement continues inside a narrow permitted path
-> one distant world fragment appears
-> the figure remains spatially separated from it
```

Use long holds and small human motion. The black field reads as unmeasured distance, interrupted memory, or inaccessible social space. Do not compensate by enlarging the face.

## Source B: Photographic Reconstruction Walkthrough

### Timeline observations

- **00:01-00:10, tree and umbrella walker:** compare the full phone photograph with a reconstruction consisting of a grass path island, a person, a trunk, and one or two canopy windows. Ordinary park footage becomes a spatial emblem through subtraction.
- **00:11-00:14, river witness:** the unedited high-angle plate proves that the source is casual observational footage: hard paving, flat river light, one standing person, street clutter.
- **00:15-00:23, river reconstruction:** keep only the standing person on two or three bright paving tiles and a small overhead water-reflection patch. The reflection becomes a remote ceiling or second world.
- **00:25-00:33, landscape slit:** use a very narrow vertical sky-and-field opening. Its slow width increase creates the sensation of a door without showing an actual door.
- **00:34-00:40, adult and child:** reduce a broad plaza to a small cross-shaped group of paving stones under the two figures. The support shape carries relationship and movement.
- **00:42-00:53, station:** preserve the concrete/escalator wedge and a warm upper floor window. Remove the crowd and most architecture; keep just enough pedestrian traffic to prove the fragment is live reality.
- **00:54-00:60, lawn:** retain a bright green ground rectangle under one figure and a separate overhead skylight-like aperture. Top and bottom light planes compress a whole park into a vertical void.
- **00:62-00:79, bench:** isolate the sitter, bench footprint, and bin footprint. Add a thin horizontal strip of park activity behind the head and shoulders. The person is present but socially disconnected from the world strip.

### Reconstruction sequence

Use this order when converting a real photograph or plate:

1. Identify the ordinary human action.
2. Preserve the smallest support surface that proves weight.
3. Preserve one object that defines the task or relationship.
4. Preserve one distant fragment that answers where the action belongs.
5. Remove competing people, clutter, and connective background.
6. Recheck perspective inside every surviving fragment.
7. Add only a restrained light fringe where the retained pixels are actually bright.

## Shared Visual Grammar

- Let black occupy most of the frame. Evidence ranges from a large majority to near-total coverage, but coverage is subordinate to legibility.
- Keep one high-value human task: walking, waiting, sitting, looking, escorting, descending, or crossing.
- Preserve the support plane before the full setting.
- Use ordinary phone or documentary photography. Compression, modest dynamic range, imperfect weather, and unstyled clothing help the image remain real.
- Compose with rectangles, wedges, paving-tile crosses, thin horizontal strips, tiny vertical windows, and irregular ground islands.
- Use scale contrast: a very small figure against a large black field, a monumental object against a tiny person, or a distant bright rectangle against a close support island.
- Hold fragments in their original lens perspective. Black may disconnect them, but it must not make their internal geometry inconsistent.
- Let movement continue inside the surviving fragments. A walking cycle across a narrow tile or road strip is more effective than a static cutout with generic glow.
- Let the last revealed fragment change the interpretation of earlier fragments: a sky window turns tiles into a room; a road wedge turns a slit into distance; a bench strip turns a silhouette into waiting.

## Two Black Operating Modes

### Spatial matte reveal

Use when black enters, opens, closes, or changes during a shot. Give it a source or readable boundary behavior, an aperture path, timing, and a landing state.

### Editorial dead-black reconstruction

Use when the result is a designed still or a reconstructed photographic tableau. Black is deliberate deletion, not an object inside the world. It does not need a diegetic source. Instead, require:

- coherent surviving photographic fragments;
- a readable support island or path;
- strong scale hierarchy;
- stable black-to-fragment boundaries;
- an intentional relation among separated fragments.

Do not force a door, shadow, curtain, or portal explanation onto editorial dead black.

## False Glitch Signals

The cited work is often tagged as glitch art, but its reusable mechanism is discontinuous continuity, not electronic malfunction. Use temporal dropping, fragment repetition, crop displacement, scale discontinuity, and missing context. Do not add RGB splitting, CRT scanlines, pixel sorting, datamosh, noisy chromatic aberration, or random compression blocks unless a new reference explicitly shows them.

## Transfer To Colored 2D Characters In Real Plates

Keep the photographic world dominant in material truth even when little of it remains visible.

- Preserve a real support fragment under the 2D character: wet pavement, grass, steps, bench, floor tile, road, or luggage contact.
- Keep rain, dust, leaves, reflections, and foreground objects photographic; let some cross in front of the drawing.
- Keep the character fully colored with unstable hand-drawn contour, matte fills, grouped hair masses, restrained cel shadow, and local hatching. Do not turn her into a white paper cutout.
- At distant scale, prioritize silhouette, posture, hand-prop tension, and support contact over eye detail.
- Let the character's line color receive the plate temperature while remaining visibly drawn.
- When the black is editorial, cast shadow and reflection stop at the visible support fragment; do not project physically into the void.
- When the black is spatial, allow the aperture to affect what part of the drawing and plate can be seen.
- Do not invent extra men, boys, companions, or protagonists. Preserve the requested subject count and gender.

## Prompt-Level Evidence Vocabulary

Prefer executable descriptions:

```text
an ordinary documentary photograph
a distant full-body figure occupying less than one tenth of frame height
a small real paving island preserving her weight and contact shadow
a narrow road wedge maintaining the source perspective
two disconnected photographic windows from the same location
near-total neutral black deleting connective context
subtle light falloff only around truly bright retained surfaces
movement continuing inside the surviving fragment
unretouched material texture, natural exposure, modest phone-camera compression
```

Avoid empty labels such as `cinematic glitch`, `surreal portal`, `digital art`, `dark atmosphere`, or `Vlad style` when geometry, scale, support, and exposure have not been specified.
