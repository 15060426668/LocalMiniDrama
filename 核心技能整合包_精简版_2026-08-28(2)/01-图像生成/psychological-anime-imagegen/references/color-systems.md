# Color Systems

Use one palette family, then assign color by material, depth, and emotional role.

## Palette anchors

For every image define:

1. skin and face midtone;
2. hair mass color;
3. environment-local colors;
4. motivated highlight or sky/window color;
5. shadow bias;
6. one small narrative accent.

Do not grade every surface with one global hue.

## Palette families

### `COLOR-SUMMER-ROAD`

- Skin: pale warm ivory with light peach cheeks.
- Hair: near-black charcoal, warm brown, or clear yellow-blonde blocks.
- Environment: dense bottle green, leaf emerald, cool concrete grey, muted red roof.
- Highlight: cyan-white overcast sky.
- Shadow: cool green-grey.
- Accent: school-bag red, lavender, coral, or pale yellow.

Use for walking home, playgrounds, pipes, slopes, and group scenes.

### `COLOR-CYAN-SKY`

- Skin: neutral pale cream.
- Hair: large clean black silhouettes.
- Environment: saturated cyan sky, soft white clouds, restrained navy clothing.
- Highlight: pale sky rim.
- Shadow: soft neutral grey with faint cyan contamination.
- Accent: one deep blue garment or warm cheek mark.

Use for profile two-shots and direct gaze tension.

### `COLOR-SCHOOL-MUTED`

- Skin: pale beige with quiet pink cheek marks.
- Hair: charcoal black or muted brown.
- Environment: faded teal doorframes, dusty salmon walls, cream ceiling, medium wood floor.
- Highlight: cool window white.
- Shadow: grey-green corridor falloff.
- Accent: one clean pink mascot or small school poster.

Use for corridors, classrooms, and subdued institutional spaces.

### `COLOR-WARM-WALL`

- Skin: warm pale peach.
- Hair: black and ochre-brown.
- Environment: soft off-white wall, dark navy clothing, weak cool edge shadow.
- Highlight: warm reflected daylight.
- Shadow: light brown-grey.
- Accent: small red or pink object.

Use for intimate indoor relationship frames.

### `COLOR-PSYCHO-PRESSURE`

- Skin: pale desaturated beige with readable mids.
- Hair: deep brown-black.
- Environment: muted olive, grey-cyan, brown-grey, dull violet.
- Highlight: narrow pale window or sky reflection.
- Shadow: localized green-brown or grey-violet around eyes, hair, neck, and frame edges.
- Accent: one distant saturated object tied to the cause of pressure.

Use for shame, dread, exhaustion, or emotional collapse.

### `COLOR-SURREAL-PINK`

- Human world: use one ordinary palette above.
- Mascot: clean coral-pink or bubblegum-pink flat fill.
- Outline: slightly darker pink or charcoal.
- Face marks: two dark dots and one tiny mouth.
- Separation: maintain a clear value or hue difference from skin and clothing.

## Emotional transition rules

- Keep ordinary daylight visible during social cruelty; environmental normality increases the emotional contrast.
- Darken the victim locally through hair shadow, foreground occlusion, or wall shade instead of turning the entire image into night.
- Increase hatching and lower saturation in close-ups before changing the whole palette.
- Preserve pale skin midtones so fatigue remains readable.
- Use magenta-heavy or purple-heavy grading only when the user explicitly selects the outlier reference.

## Color gate

Verify:

- skin, hair, environment, source, and shadow have separate roles;
- greens retain at least two values in outdoor scenes;
- black hair keeps shape separation from the background;
- the accent occupies a small region;
- psychological color remains localized;
- every named color describes a visible region.
