## Core Formula

`GPT composition skill = knowledge feed + framework constraint + self-iteration`

The manual's central claim is practical: do not treat "training" as model retraining. Treat it as structured, repeatable feeding:

- feed professional composition knowledge
- constrain the analysis/output framework
- build a feedback loop that scores, diagnoses, revises, and deposits reusable rules

## Knowledge Feed Package

Use a layered feed order:

1. Basic concepts: composition rules, shot language, light, color.
2. Analysis framework: three-layer analysis, seven-step workflow, scoring standard.
3. Case library: positive cases, negative cases, comparison cases, scene-specific cases.
4. Feedback standard: quality scoring and acceptance rules.

Feed by modules. Do not dump unstructured theory into the skill.

## Foundation Library

### Composition Rules

- Three-point / rule of thirds: place subjects on intersections to create natural balance; useful for portraits, dialogue, object emphasis.
- Symmetry: creates stability, ritual, authority, institutional order, or uncanny control.
- Leading lines: corridors, roads, rails, shadows, architecture, gaze direction; guides attention into the subject.
- Frame-within-frame: doors, windows, shelves, bodies, reflections; creates containment, secrecy, distance, or voyeurism.
- Golden spiral: organic eye path, elegant natural flow; useful for character focus and still life.
- Negative space: isolation, pressure, absence, waiting, inner emptiness.
- Foreground composition: adds depth, secrecy, peeking, immersion, or surveillance.
- Dutch angle: instability, confusion, threat, mental imbalance; use sparingly and with cause.

### Shot Language

- Extreme long shot / long shot: environment dominates; loneliness, scale, awe, social context.
- Full shot: whole body and space relation; objective action or staging.
- Medium shot: waist/knee-up human interaction; dialogue and readable gestures.
- Close shot: chest-up emotional concentration; reaction and face behavior.
- Close-up / insert: face, eyes, object, hand, clue; strong emotion or narrative proof.
- Low angle: power, pressure, authority, threat, heroic exaggeration.
- High angle: vulnerability, weakness, surveillance, divine or systemic view.
- Eye-level: equality, realism, emotional honesty, everyday contact.

### Light And Color

Treat light as narrative pressure, not decoration.

- Key-light direction: side light, backlight, top light, bottom light, Rembrandt light.
- Light quality: hard light for conflict, threat, drama; soft light for intimacy, memory, romance, gentleness.
- Color temperature: cool for calm, alienation, oppression, sci-fi; warm for memory, intimacy, hope.
- Contrast: high contrast for conflict and tension; low saturation for realism, depression, exhaustion.
- Classic palettes: teal/orange contrast, black-and-white severity, single-color stylization.

## Case Library Method

The manual recommends three case types:

- Positive cases: what works and why.
- Negative cases: what fails and why.
- Comparison cases: same scene, good version vs weak version.

For each case, record:

- scene and narrative purpose
- image structure
- composition rule
- lens / camera / shot size
- light direction and color
- why it works
- how to improve or transfer it

## Three-Layer Analysis

Use this for every film frame or shot-design task.

### Narrative Layer

Ask:

- What story information does this frame carry?
- What is the emotional tone?
- What is the character relationship?
- Who has power, who is watched, who is hidden?

### Visual Layer

Ask:

- Which composition rules are active?
- What shot size, camera height, angle, lens, and depth are implied?
- What foreground, midground, and background layers exist?
- What light direction, contrast, color, and texture carry the emotion?
- How does movement or stillness affect attention?

### Language Layer

Ask:

- How should this be written as a storyboard?
- How should it be written as an SD/Seedance prompt?
- Which visible anchors reduce model ambiguity?
- Which terms are too abstract and need visible replacement?

## Seven-Step Composition Workflow

1. Analyze narrative need: emotion, character, relationship, event, information release.
2. Determine shot parameters: shot size, camera height, viewing angle, focal length, depth of field.
3. Choose composition rules: 1-3 rules that serve the scene.
4. Design lighting: key direction, light quality, contrast, practical sources.
5. Set color style: palette, saturation, temperature, symbolic color anchors.
6. Generate full plan: structured composition design or prompt.
7. Quality self-check: score and revise before delivery.

## Six-Dimension Scoring Standard

Use a 10-point score per dimension, with weighted total:

- Visual narrative: 25%. Does the composition accurately communicate emotion and story?
- Composition rule use: 20%. Are rules applied correctly and does the frame feel balanced or intentionally imbalanced?
- Shot-language accuracy: 20%. Do shot size, camera angle, and focal length match the narrative need?
- Light atmosphere: 15%. Does the light design create the intended mood and remain physically plausible?
- Spatial layering: 10%. Are foreground, midground, and background clear?
- Style consistency: 10%. Does the frame match the story world and reference style?

Passing target:

- 8.0+ for normal output.
- 8.5+ for final SD/Seedance prompt.
- If any dimension falls under 7, revise before presenting as final.

## Scene Variants

### Character Close-Up

Add:

- emotional state
- key facial region: eyes, mouth, profile, hands
- background relationship: blank, symbolic, blurred, oppressive, flowing
- eye light and shadow placement

### Establishing Shot

Add:

- space scale: grand, narrow, open, oppressive
- time and weather
- crowd or environmental motion
- social hierarchy and spatial power

### Dialogue Scene

Add:

- relationship: hostile, intimate, awkward, superior/subordinate
- dialogue emotion: testing, denial, confession, threat
- camera logic: shot/reverse-shot, over-shoulder, eye-level, high/low angle, blocking triangle
- background motion must continue if the environment is alive

### Action Scene

Add:

- action type: chase, impact, stumble, fall, grab, run
- rhythm: fast, slow, burst, held tension
- movement device: handheld, tracking, fixed, slow motion, whip pan
- force direction and screen direction

## Self-Training Loop

Each training cycle should perform:

1. Generate a composition plan.
2. Score it with the six-dimension standard.
3. Diagnose weak points.
4. Revise the plan.
5. Re-score.
6. Deposit 1-3 reusable principles into the case library.

## Failure Diagnosis

Common failures and fixes:

- Vague composition: add exact camera height, subject position, screen side, and layer structure.
- Wrong shot size or angle: tie camera position to the emotional function.
- Flat image: add foreground, midground, background, or blocking depth.
- Weak lighting: specify key direction, source, quality, contrast, and color temperature.
- Style drift: describe visual properties instead of relying on vague style labels.
- AIGC ambiguity: convert abstract intent into visible facts and remove contradictory instructions.
