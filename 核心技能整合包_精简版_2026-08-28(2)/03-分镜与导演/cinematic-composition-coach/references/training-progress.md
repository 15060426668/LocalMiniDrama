### 2026-06-25 - Round 3: Four User-Fed Composition Practice Set

The user fed four reference frames and requested a three-layer self-analysis, self-audit, correction, and knowledge-base deposit. Added four formal entries to `composition-case-library.md`:

- Case 12: mirror / doorway / split family witness frame.
- Case 13: window-grid silhouette / aftermath bond against the city.
- Case 14: frontal domestic symmetry / vertical family hierarchy tableau.
- Case 15: apocalyptic smoke-column silhouette / witnesses against disaster scale.

Corrections learned:

- Mirror composition is not automatically symmetry; ask what the reflection reveals that the body hides.
- Silhouette window shots need the outside world to carry consequence; otherwise they become decorative skyline frames.
- Frontal domestic tableau must lock hierarchy through vertical levels, rows, props, and color balance.
- Catastrophe reveal often works better through distant witness scale, smoke rhythm, and sound restraint than through large close explosions.
- AIGC prompt transfer must lock reflective identity, body scale, figure direction, and foreground/midground/background physics.

### 2026-06-25 - Round 4: Corrective Training From Failed Ship Farewell

The user rejected the ship-pier farewell draft as ordinary, with equal character scale, weak distance, weak staging, and insufficient use of learned film grammar. Added corrective rules to `references/researched-composition-expansion.md`:

- Case R13: Godfather-inspired low-key information control.
- Case R14: hero entrance / delayed reveal and silhouette scale.
- Case R15: accident / danger staging through causal chain and center clarity.
- Case R16: extreme farewell distance / unequal scale and irreversible transit.

Corrections learned:

- A farewell frame must not give both characters equal size, equal clarity, equal light, and equal visual weight.
- Physical separation must be proven by visible irreversible actions: raised gangway, slack rope, closing door, hull blocking sightline, dark wake cutting reflection, crowd/security line.
- Low-key lighting means controlling information, not simply darkening the whole image; choose what body part or prop receives light.
- Hero entrance requires delayed reveal, backlight, a spatial gap being filled, and group/space reaction; a lone pose is not enough.
- Dangerous accident staging must preserve a clear causal chain and center clarity; chaos without cause reads as noise.
