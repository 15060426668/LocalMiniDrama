# Director Dispatch

Use this router to rank film-reference mechanisms, choose or combine useful director approaches, and establish one coherent grammar for the current short. Detailed execution remains in specialist references.

## Core Rule

Each candidate option explains a reusable director mechanism. Rank several options and let the user filter or combine them. The approved synthesis then redesigns camera, staging, rhythm, sound and transitions for the current duration; no film owns the short's timing.

## Fast Diagnosis

| Scene engine | Primary grammar | Required proof |
|---|---|---|
| Audience knows danger before the character | Hitchcock | visible information gap, delayed character access, object or threshold carrier |
| Ordinary reality becomes subtly wrong | Lynch | stable normal baseline, one precise drift, sensory residue |
| Evidence, procedure, institution or system controls people | Fincher | readable proof plane, controlled geometry, dark detail |
| Rule, time, nested layer or physical concept drives pressure | Nolan | rule taught physically, scale anchored to human/object consequence |
| Dialogue, etiquette, gesture or prop delays rupture | Tarantino | room geography, hidden power shift, trigger and aftermath |

## Hitchcock

Use for information-gap suspense, voyeurism, object anxiety and delayed reveal.

- Core equation: viewpoint + information permission + delayed reaction.
- Composition: foreground barrier, watched subject, readable danger/object in another layer; doors, windows, mirrors, screens and stairs carry access control.
- Camera: POV/reverse POV, object insert, slow push, locked waiting, high vulnerability angle, low threat angle; reaction and hand/object close-ups often outrank action coverage.
- Lens tendency: `35-50mm` observation, `85-135mm` compression, `24-28mm` subjective vertigo.
- Light: motivated hard side/back/single-source light; bright zone carries known proof, shadow carries withheld information; typical high contrast `8:1-12:1` with recoverable dark detail.
- Sound: silence or off-screen cue changes what the viewer knows before the character reacts.
- Failure: simultaneous reveal to viewer and character, decorative silhouette/blinds, camera motion without an information job, dead black that hides evidence.

## Lynch

Use for uncanny normality, dream dread, identity drift and subconscious clues.

- Core equation: ordinary baseline -> precise sensory violation -> unresolved residue.
- Composition: understandable room first; over-perfect symmetry, misplaced emptiness, low ordinary objects and thresholds become subtly wrong.
- Camera: stillness held past comfort, slow threshold push, overly smooth travel, sound-led reveal, focus/exposure drift.
- Lens tendency: `35-50mm` normal baseline, `18-28mm` domestic distortion, `85-200mm` inaccessible faces.
- Light: mixed or impossible source logic, fake warmth, red/blue/green/magenta ownership, overexposed threshold or irregular fluorescent drift. Wrong light matters more than generic darkness.
- Sound: low-frequency room tone, impossible pre-lap, ritualized ordinary noise.
- Failure: random symbols, no normal baseline, total spatial unreadability, color pile-up, early obvious weirdness, decorative flicker.

## Fincher

Use for evidence pressure, institutions, procedural control, screens/files and relationship systems.

- Core equation: system geometry + readable evidence + controlled behavior.
- Composition: architecture, desks, screens, glass, files and corridors overpower people; proof planes remain flat and legible; symmetry implies control or performance.
- Camera: exact eye-level or slight surveillance angle, locked observation, mechanical push/follow, evidence scan, screen-within-screen.
- Lens tendency: `35-50mm` procedure, `24-32mm` system architecture, `85-135mm` evidence/surveillance compression.
- Light: cold low-saturation practicals, top/side-top fluorescent or screen light, usually `6:1-10:1`; shadows retain evidence-level detail.
- Sound: routine, machinery and interface cues reinforce system ownership.
- Failure: dark teal as decoration, evidence lost in shadow, handheld chaos before geography, symmetry without control meaning, skin rendered accidentally sick.

## Nolan

Use for structural suspense, rules, countdowns, nested layers, physicalized concepts and scale.

- Core equation: clear physical rule -> escalating cross-effect -> human/object cost.
- Composition: center axis, repeated geometry, thresholds, nested frames and scale hierarchy teach the rule.
- Camera: establish geography and proof first; motivated movement through architecture; scale shot returns to face, hand, clock or object consequence.
- Lens tendency: wide for scale, normal for rule clarity, longer for human cost; focal behavior stays physically plausible unless the rule changes it.
- Light: practical motivated hard light, neutral-cool world with one meaningful warm human/object accent; broad dynamic detail, usually `6:1-10:1`.
- Sound: clocks, mechanical rhythm, cross-layer bridges and image/sound delay express structure.
- Failure: complexity before teaching, spectacle without cost, cross-cut layers that do not affect one another, giant space without a human anchor.

## Tarantino

Use for dialogue standoff, social pressure, ritual delay and gesture/object-triggered reversal.

- Core equation: room geography + performance rhythm + delayed trigger.
- Composition: two-shots and wides preserve power distance; table, hand, drink, key, phone, shoes or etiquette become trigger carriers.
- Camera: slow lateral observation, held dialogue frame, push only on power change, sudden rupture after mature delay.
- Lens tendency: normal/short telephoto confrontation, wider room staging, low-angle wider threat.
- Light: theatrical motivated warm hard light, saturated accent with rich blacks, often `8:1-12:1`; shadow cuts and haze require a credible source.
- Sound: music, silence, object foley and speech pauses form timing architecture.
- Failure: close-up ping-pong, rupture too early, decorative prop, dialogue without power change, unmotivated stage light or heavy grain.

## Per-Segment Director Synthesis

- A scheme pool may contain different directors and films; options do not need to match the current segment duration.
- Filtering may select one option or combine named mechanisms from several options.
- Before formal storyboarding, resolve conflicts and state one primary director grammar.
- The approved formal storyboard becomes the sole execution source for Dream SD.
- The next segment may reuse or change references while inheriting continuity from the approved storyboard tail.

## Anti-Mixing

- Talking does not automatically mean Tarantino; speech must function as a power weapon.
- Complexity does not automatically mean Nolan; a rule, clock or physical system must drive suspense.
- Strange imagery does not automatically mean Lynch; normal reality must first become unstable.
- Dark color does not automatically mean Fincher; precision, evidence or system pressure must own the frame.
- Danger does not automatically mean Hitchcock; information timing must be the engine.
