# Dream / Reality Motion Mechanisms

Use this reference for repeated waking, reality resets, associative dream pursuit, cross-space synchronized triggers, rotating corridors, gravity changes, stable-layout identity drift, self-reconstruction scans, reflection desynchronization, memory-space extinction, and dream-to-reality residue.

Source normalization: distilled from user-supplied generated training batches. Named-film timing, focal lengths, light percentages, sound frequencies and invented micro-actions remain C-grade film facts. The mechanism families below are retained as B-grade transferable grammar until verified against footage.

## 1. Repeated Wake / Reality Reset

Core engine:

```text
stable frame and body action
-> one changed reality proof
-> delayed recognition
-> body or object verification
-> residue that survives the next reset
```

Lock across resets:

- camera side, height, framing family and screen position.
- the first body action: sit up, reach, inhale, touch face, grip bedding or scan the room.
- one recurring sound signature.
- one identity anchor and one carried residue.

Change one dominant proof per reset:

- room geometry or doorway location.
- bedding, wardrobe or body residue.
- one object state, owner or material.
- motivated light source or sound-source permission.
- background task or institutional clue.

Readable order:

```text
wake action repeats -> audience notices the changed proof -> character notices later
-> hand/gaze verifies it -> tail frame preserves the new evidence
```

Short-form rule: establish the baseline once, use the next reset for a clear mismatch, and use the tail for evidence rather than another full explanation.

Failure boundary:

- Fully identical resets feel repetitive; fully redesigned resets lose the comparison.
- Several changed props, wardrobe, light and room geometry in the same beat weaken the evidence hierarchy.
- A reset without residue becomes a disconnected surprise rather than a reality-layer mechanism.

SD anchors: exact matched camera relation, repeated body action, changed proof, verifying hand/gaze, recurring sound tail, final residue.

## 2. Motion-Invariant Associative Scene Shift

Core engine:

```text
one uninterrupted objective and movement vector
-> occluder, object contact or material boundary
-> environment changes around the motion
-> cadence and camera relation continue
-> old space leaves one residual trace
```

Lock the motion bus:

- subject direction, cadence and center-of-gravity behavior.
- camera vector, speed relationship and landing distance.
- one contact anchor: handrail, wall, floor, doorway, vehicle handle or carried prop.
- one sound pulse that continues through the transition.
- one attention owner and one readable destination.

Change only the environment family in the transition beat. Let the new ground, wall, light or prop inherit a line, texture, shape, motion direction or sound from the old space.

Transition grammar:

```text
old surface/occluder -> fills or crosses frame -> visible boundary behavior
-> new surface inherits direction/pattern -> residual trace -> movement continues
```

For one 15-second AIGC segment, two or three readable spaces are normally stronger than a long parade of unrelated locations.

Failure boundary:

- Restarting the actor's gait or camera path after every change turns continuity into montage.
- Several matter families in one transition create random morphing.
- A pursuit without an objective, distance relation or landing frame becomes decorative motion.

SD anchors: movement direction, gait/contact physics, exact camera path, transition surface, single matter family, inherited pattern, sound bridge, landing image.

## 3. Cross-Space Synchronized Trigger

Core engine:

```text
each space establishes its own force and action phase
-> one shared trigger approaches
-> cuts align equivalent movement phases
-> local consequences land differently
-> one layer wakes, releases or changes the next
```

Build a card for every active layer:

```text
space identity / local force / character objective / action phase
/ trigger source / sound signature / contact point / landing state
```

Synchronization is owned by action phase, force direction or sound pulse, not by identical faces or identical props. A fall may match a chair drop, elevator impact, bed collapse or hand release when their causal phase is equivalent.

Rhythm rule: establish the layers with longer readable beats, shorten the intercut interval as the shared trigger approaches, then hold long enough to show each local consequence.

Short-form rule: two layers are the stable default; use a third only when each space has a distinct color, sound and physical carrier.

Failure boundary:

- Eye close-ups alone do not identify space or cause.
- Simultaneous impacts without a visible trigger graph feel arbitrary.
- Film-scale layer counts and edit density overload a short segment.

SD anchors: layer identity, local force, shared action phase, trigger sound, contact point, consequence, layer-to-layer tail inheritance.

## 4. Rotating Corridor / Gravity Reorientation

Track five coordinate facts:

```text
camera frame / architectural frame / gravity vector
/ body support surface / loose-object direction
```

Readable order:

```text
loose object proves the new gravity
-> feet lose support
-> hand, shoulder or back contacts the next surface
-> that surface becomes the new floor
-> knees/feet absorb the landing
-> residue settles in the same direction
```

Choose one camera relationship per beat:

- camera fixed to the architecture: the set appears stable while the body reorients.
- camera fixed to the outside world: the architecture visibly rotates.
- camera follows the body: use only after gravity direction is already established.

Physical proof must include force direction, lost support, contact point, resistance, secondary inertia, recovery step and settling state. Loose props, clothing, hair, dust and practical lights all answer to the same gravity owner.

AIGC stable coverage:

```text
beat 1: architecture tilts and loose prop moves
beat 2: feet slide and hand/shoulder catches the wall
beat 3: wall becomes floor and body lands
```

Failure boundary:

- A 360-degree rotation, fight choreography, floating debris, lighting failure and wardrobe transformation in one beat exceed readable model load.
- Mixing camera-fixed, world-fixed and body-fixed orientation inside one beat destroys spatial proof.
- Gravity change without contact and settling looks like weightless animation.

SD anchors: rotation axis, gravity direction, camera relation, support surface, contact chain, loose-object proof, practical-light orientation, final landing pose.

## 5. Stable Layout / Identity Drift

Core engine:

```text
stable spatial checksum and repeated action route
-> one layout, ownership or identity variable changes
-> audience or character detects the mismatch late
-> body, object, sound or threshold verifies it
-> the next repetition preserves one residue or returns one anchor
```

Build the checksum from no more than four anchors:

- camera side, height and framing family.
- one repeated body route: sit, cross room, open door, enter bedroom, answer knock or look in mirror.
- one owner object or sound: cup, watch, blanket, radio phrase, knock rhythm or appliance cycle.
- one spatial relation: sofa-to-door distance, bed orientation, doorway side, window-light direction or table ownership.

Change one dominant variable per beat:

- furniture location or room function.
- person identity, age, wardrobe or relationship claim.
- object owner, material or written label.
- light/source permission or recurring sound source.

The strongest version lets the unchanged anchors remain plausible long enough that the mismatch is discovered rather than announced.

Failure boundary:

- Changing layout, identity, costume, light and every prop together destroys comparison.
- A dialogue declaration such as “this is not my home” cannot replace the physical checksum.
- Returning to baseline without one carried doubt, object state, body reaction or sound residue erases the mechanism.

SD anchors: matched camera relation, repeated body route, four-item spatial checksum, single changed variable, delayed gaze/hand verification, identity or ownership proof, residue.

## 6. Evidence Scan / Self-Reconstruction

Use when a character wakes without reliable memory, enters an unfamiliar room, suspects identity replacement or must reconstruct what happened from the body and nearby evidence.

Readable scan order:

```text
breath / pain / balance
-> body marks and wardrobe state
-> immediate support surface and room exits
-> carried or pocket objects
-> written, photographic or recorded clue
-> off-screen visitor / threshold pressure
-> present choice based on incomplete evidence
```

The order is causal, not decorative. Survival and body ownership come before room explanation; room permission comes before interpreting remote evidence. Focus, gaze and hand movement should pass the information baton in the same order.

For each 4-15 second dramatic phase inside a 4-30 second segment, keep three or four proofs: one body proof, one room proof, one object clue and one interrupted choice. Longer evidence chains become repetitive insert montages.

Failure boundary:

- Random close-ups of tattoos, photographs, notes and weapons do not create deduction unless each changes the next action.
- The character cannot understand a clue before establishing the ability to read, remember or trust it.
- A phone call or visitor should interrupt a current inference, not arrive as unrelated exposition.

SD anchors: awakening body action, pain/mark location, gaze route, hand-to-object ownership, room exit map, focus handoff, clue legibility, interruption source, final choice.

## 7. Reflection Desync / Body Intrusion

Core engine:

```text
normal reflection synchrony
-> one minimal lag, direction error or independent gaze
-> character repeats or tests the action
-> reflection or body gives one physical proof
-> anomaly escalates locally
-> aftermath remains on skin, support object, mirror surface or behavior
```

Establish normal synchrony before breaking it. Choose one anomaly family per beat:

- temporal lag or premature completion.
- left/right or rotation-direction disagreement.
- reflection gaze targets something the body does not see.
- localized body mark, texture, support pressure or movement appears outside conscious control.

Keep camera, mirror plane, actor position and focus readable enough to verify the mismatch. If the audience cannot compare body and reflection, the effect becomes ambiguous compositing noise.

Failure boundary:

- Do not combine lag, duplicate body, changing face, feathers, cracks and independent movement in one short beat.
- Body transformation without touch, weight, pain, material response or later trace feels like surface morphing.
- A mirror anomaly that never changes the character's next test or action is decorative.

SD anchors: baseline synchronized action, mirror plane, camera side, focus depth, single mismatch, repeat-test gesture, contact proof, localized material change, aftermath.

## 8. Memory-Space Extinction

Use when a remembered place is erased, forgotten, censored or collapses while the character tries to retain one person, object or feeling.

Readable extinction order:

```text
stable memory anchor
-> semantic detail disappears first
-> object or boundary loses material completeness
-> light and sound permission narrow
-> space becomes blank, dark or physically absent
-> last person/object anchor remains briefly
-> final anchor disappears or crosses into the return frame
```

Possible semantic-first proofs include text losing letters, a face losing recognizable detail, a familiar sound losing words, a shelf losing labels or an object losing ownership. Let erasure travel through one material family and direction rather than deleting the whole world at once.

The character must attempt a counteraction: grip, carry, hide, repeat a name, cross into an older memory or preserve one sound/object. The failed or partial attempt creates emotion and gives the transition a physical route.

Failure boundary:

- Instant whiteout or random environmental disappearance has no readable loss hierarchy.
- Too many memory locations in one short segment become a trailer montage rather than an inhabited collapse.
- Erasure without a retained feeling, gesture, object state or sound tail has no residue and therefore no dramatic consequence.

SD anchors: memory anchor, erasure source and direction, first semantic loss, material family, character counteraction, last surviving anchor, transition surface, return-frame residue.

## 9. Non-Combat Doppelganger

Use when another self should create identity pressure without becoming a fight scene.

```text
shared costume/body marker proves identity
-> both bodies perform one simple synchronized action
-> one minimal mismatch appears
-> protagonist repeats the action as a test
-> double makes one independent choice
-> ownership, route, or observer knowledge changes
```

Keep both bodies comparable through a doorway plane, reflection, corridor depth, or foreground/background staging. Choose one mismatch family per beat: lag, direction, gaze target, object ownership, or route permission. Preserve camera side and body marker long enough for verification.

Failure boundary:

- Immediate combat destroys the identity question before it develops.
- Multiple glitches, face morphs and wardrobe changes at once look like unstable generation rather than a rule.
- The repeat-test must produce a new choice; otherwise the mismatch is decorative.

SD anchors: shared marker, baseline action, comparison plane, single mismatch, repeat-test, independent action, changed ownership/route, tail state.

## 10. Repeated Space / Background Witness

Use when a familiar room, corridor, doorway or family layout changes while a background person may know more than the protagonist.

```text
stable frame and routine action
-> one spatial variable changes
-> protagonist discovers it through use
-> protagonist retests or reverses the action
-> background witness reveals a knowledge state
-> new layout changes the next route
```

Lock camera side, doorway, major furniture axis, light direction and protagonist route. Change one variable: handedness, door side, object owner, witness position, or passage direction. Assign the witness one knowledge state: unaware, suppressing recognition, anticipating the test, or correcting behavior after exposure.

Failure boundary:

- Many drifting props read as continuity failure.
- A witness who only stares does not carry information.
- The protagonist must retest intelligently instead of accepting the change for plot convenience.

SD anchors: stable frame checksum, single changed variable, discovery action, retest route, witness micro-action, invariant object/sound, new exit state.

## 11. Shared Rules

1. Preserve one invariant bus and change one dominant evidence family at a time.
2. Let the audience detect a physical discrepancy before dialogue explains it.
3. Make sound trigger body, focus, camera, edit, light or space.
4. Carry residue across the transition: wetness, dust, grip, fabric state, object orientation, light state or sound tail.
5. Build the current short's timing independently; film pacing and generated training-table durations never own the edit.
6. Keep named-film provenance upstream. After director synthesis, the approved formal storyboard becomes the only SD source.
