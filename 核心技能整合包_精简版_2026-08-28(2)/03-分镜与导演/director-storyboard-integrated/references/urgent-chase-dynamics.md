# Urgent Chase Dynamics

Use this file when a scene needs urgent chase, panic escape, frantic door action, being pursued, corridor pressure, sudden look-back, low-angle running, breath-driven fear, or when the user says the motion feels calm, flat, slow, or lacking pressure.

## Source Mechanisms

- `The Shining` Big Wheel / maze logic: low lens height, rapid following, corridor repetition, floor material sound changes, the camera becomes the pursuing presence.
- `[REC]` live-report logic: camera stays reactive to events, body movement pulls the frame, panic is carried by imperfect framing, off-screen noise, and breathing.
- `A Quiet Place` sound logic: tiny close-up sounds become survival pressure; footsteps, fabric, breath, and object knocks are mixed by distance and perspective.
- `It Follows` pressure logic: the pursuer may move slowly, but it stays present in deep background, so the audience scans the whole frame before the character understands.
- `Se7en` rain-chase logic: weather, wet ground, alleys, obstacles, and hard foot impacts make the pursuit tactile instead of abstract.
- Fast chase craft: narrow aisles, wall lines, foreground obstacles, passing light patches, and static pass-by shots increase perceived velocity through parallax.
- Short-form AIGC rhythm: one urgent beat every 1-3 seconds; fixed images refresh through impact, light flicker, body recoil, prop collision, focus pull, or cut.

## Psychological Horror Chase Layer

Use this layer when the chase should feel oppressive, uncanny, and psychologically terrifying rather than like clean action choreography.

Core principle:

```text
no escape pressure -> space hostility -> body breakdown -> active background -> sound pre-pressure -> false safety -> worse proximity
```

Key rules:

- The chase engine is `nowhere to escape`, not only speed. A calm pursuer, an unseen voice, or a distant figure can feel stronger than a fast attacker if the space keeps returning them to the character.
- Treat the space as the enemy: repeating doors, symmetrical corridors, maze turns, dead-end light, low ceilings, wet floor reflections, and converging wall lines should press toward the subject.
- Use deep focus when the threat must be searched for. Keep background readable so the audience scans doorways, hallway ends, reflections, and crowd gaps.
- Body panic must leave physical traces: stumble, wall touch, misstep, hand slip, door impact, bag swing, hair lag, cloth snag, object knock, breath break.
- Background must keep acting. People react, freeze, avoid, stare, continue routines, block the path, or become indifferent witnesses. Objects swing, fall, roll, flicker, drip, slide, or echo after contact.
- Sound should arrive before proof: footstep, scrape, whisper, breathing, fluorescent buzz change, rain hit, object fall, or a voice behind the subject should pull the camera or body reaction 1-3 seconds before visual confirmation.
- Use wave rhythm: sprint -> brief hiding/stillness -> false relief -> sound/object cue -> threat feels closer -> renewed sprint.
- The pursuer can remain in the depth of field instead of appearing by surprise. The audience sees a small distant pressure while the character misses it.
- Physical contact proves weight. Every urgent beat should include a body-to-space contact point: floor, wall, door, handle, furniture, bag strap, coat edge, puddle, glass, or curtain.

Psychological chase row gate:

```text
threat distance -> audience awareness vs character awareness -> space pressure -> body failure -> background reaction -> sound pre-pressure -> tail-frame danger
```

## Dynamic Engine

Urgent chase is built from four moving layers:

```text
body force -> camera force -> space force -> sound force
```

- Body force: stumble, shoulder hit, hand slip, knee drive, heel-to-toe push-off, torso lean, head snap, throat swallow, bag collision, clothing recoil.
- Camera force: POV burst, impact shake, rapid lateral truck, low rear tracking, side tracking, whip pan, fixed pass-by, counter-move toward subject, crash stop.
- Space force: narrow walls rushing past, foreground frame edge streaks, lights passing overhead, floor reflections breaking under feet, doorframe / wall / pipe near-miss.
- Sound force: breath clipped short, heartbeat impact, shoes striking ground, cloth scrape, bag buckle hit, fluorescent buzz, off-screen voice, sudden sound drop, low pulse.

Every chase shot should name at least one item from each layer.

## Panic Door Action

Use for sudden opening, escape through a door, failed grip, or threshold fear.

```text
hand hits surface -> fingers search -> first grip slips -> second grip clamps -> wrist twists -> shoulder drives body weight -> door jolts open -> camera is pulled through the threshold
```

Visible details:

- palm slaps door or wall before finding handle.
- fingers scrabble across wet metal.
- wrist twists hard, tendons visible.
- shoulder or hip drives the door.
- door edge crosses close to lens.
- camera lurches with the body instead of floating smoothly.

Sound details:

- palm impact.
- fingernail scrape.
- two fast handle clicks.
- hinge shriek.
- door edge thump.
- breath catch before crossing.

## Sudden Look-Back

Use when a sound behind the character triggers fear.

```text
sound hits -> body flinches -> shoulders jerk -> eyes lead -> head snaps back -> hair lags -> camera reacts -> landing frame reveals changed space -> body recovers into forward motion
```

Camera choices:

- `rapid lateral truck / slide`: camera translates along wall line to the rear doorway or empty wall.
- `fast whip pan`: camera pivots with head movement for subjective panic.
- `POV snap back`: first-person head turn with short motion blur.

For the current corridor opening, prefer `rapid lateral truck / slide`: it reads as a fast glance plus a camera follow, while the body stays oriented forward.

## Low-Angle Running

Use when the viewer needs to feel speed through legs and floor.

```text
low angle rear tracking close to ground -> shoes strike light patches -> floor reflection breaks -> foreground wall edge streaks -> bag and coat swing -> camera pumps with footfall
```

Required physical proof:

- foot contacts ground, heel-to-toe or forefoot push-off.
- knee drives upward and forward.
- torso leans into escape.
- clothing and hair follow with delayed inertia.
- loose object hits body rhythmically.
- camera distance breathes with the runner instead of staying perfectly locked.

Speed boosters:

- narrow corridor.
- foreground walls or doorframes close to lens.
- repeating overhead lights.
- alternating bright/dark patches.
- static pass-by insert where the character blasts through frame.
- side tracking with foreground occlusion.

## Panic Performance

Panic is readable through interrupted action, not only facial fear.

Use micro-beats:

- breath catches, then breaks into short bursts.
- lips part, then clamp shut.
- throat swallows before an answer can form.
- shoulders jump at sound.
- fingers over-grip the door handle.
- one foot misplants, then corrects.
- bag hits hip and pulls the body off balance.
- head snaps back while torso stays forward.
- eyes scan faster than the head.

For trained/professional characters, panic stays functional:

- they move fast but still choose direction.
- they suppress verbal response.
- they test space with glance, foot, hand, reflection, light, or sound.
- their fear leaks through body mechanics, not screaming.

## Sound Rhythm

For a 15-second urgent segment:

```text
0-2s: body impact + object grab
2-4s: threshold burst + hinge / air change
4-7s: space opens + heartbeat accelerates
7-9s: off-screen sound hits + body flinch
9-11s: camera snap / truck + sound smear
11-15s: dense footfall + cloth + bag + low pulse + broken breath
```

Sound must follow camera distance:

- close-up: fingertips, nail scrape, swallow, breath, fabric.
- medium movement: shoe strikes, bag buckle, door hinge, wall scrape.
- wide corridor: room tone, fluorescent buzz, long echo, distant hum.
- subjective panic: heartbeat, tinnitus edge, muffled drop, low pulse.

## Formal Row Gate

For every urgent chase storyboard row, include:

- exact body force: which hand, foot, shoulder, head, or torso moves.
- contact proof: handle, floor, wall, door edge, bag, clothing, light patch.
- camera force: POV burst, handheld shake, lateral truck, low rear tracking, side tracking, whip pan, fixed pass-by.
- spatial speed proof: foreground streak, narrow wall, light patch, floor reflection, doorframe near lens.
- sound density: breath, foot, cloth, object, electric hum, off-screen pressure, heartbeat / low pulse.
- edit reason: cut on impact, cut on foot strike, cut on light flicker, cut on sound hit, cut on landing frame.
