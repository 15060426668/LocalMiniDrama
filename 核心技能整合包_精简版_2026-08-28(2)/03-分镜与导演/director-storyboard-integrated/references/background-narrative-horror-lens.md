# Background Narrative Horror Lens

Use this file when the user mentions 背景说话, 背景叙事, 非主体叙事, 背景信息差, 前景日常背景恐怖, 主角没发现观众发现, NPC微反应, 群众反应, 反射恐怖, 靠道具说话, 靠场景说话, or wants suspense carried by background details instead of the main subject.

## Core Distinction

This module is not the same as active background.

| Layer | Job |
|---|---|
| 活背景 | Make the frame feel alive: props, extras, light, sound, and space continue acting. |
| 物件语言 | Give a prop dramatic function: rule, evidence, trigger, countdown, identity, memory, tail-frame hook. |
| 背景叙事 | Let background carry information the subject does not have, so the audience discovers danger, clue, or contradiction first. |

Background narrative has two modes:

- Suspense mode: the background carries danger, clue, contradiction, or information gap.
- Narrative mode: the background carries world, time, relationship, power, emotion, irony, foreshadowing, or backstory without exposition.

Suspense mode is Hitchcock information gap pushed into the frame:

```text
foreground normal action -> background information carrier -> audience discovery window -> foreground sound masks clue -> protagonist ignorance proof -> unresolved tail frame
```

Narrative mode is environmental storytelling pushed into the frame:

```text
foreground anchor action -> background story evidence -> natural placement -> layered information -> audience inference -> later payoff
```

The strongest version keeps the foreground completely ordinary while the background quietly changes the story.

## Core Law

The frame must contain two simultaneous stories:

- Foreground story: the subject performs a normal, readable action.
- Background story: a threat, clue, contradiction, NPC reaction, prop movement, reflection, screen, or spatial detail reveals information.
- Audience position: the audience can notice the background before the subject.
- Subject position: the subject continues without noticing within that shot.
- Sound relation: foreground sound or room tone masks the background event.
- Tail frame: the shot ends with the background information unresolved.

## Background Narrative Families

| Family | Mechanism | Best Use |
|---|---|---|
| Slow background approach | A distant figure, shadow, vehicle, reflection, or silhouette approaches while foreground remains routine. | Everyday unease, domestic scenes, corridors, shops, public spaces. |
| Reflection / glass / screen reveal | The abnormal detail appears in mirror, glass, phone black screen, monitor, stainless steel, puddle, or TV reflection. | Psychological suspicion, dream pollution, hidden follower, double-self hints. |
| NPC micro-reaction | A passerby, clerk, neighbor, guard, family member, or crowd reacts to something behind or around the subject. | The audience infers threat through someone else's restraint. |
| Collective background behavior | Many extras stop, part, turn, lower their voices, or repeat one gesture while the subject moves normally. | Social pressure, system control, ritual pressure, uncanny public space. |
| Autonomous object / space change | A door lock turns, curtain shifts, fridge opens, stair lights activate, elevator number changes, or a chair moves while subject misses it. | Invisible presence, rule-object pressure, sealed-room uncertainty. |
| Look-back displacement | The subject checks one direction; when they return forward, the background has changed in the other direction. | Cognitive shock, non-jump scare, dream/reality contamination. |
| Supporting-character silent clue | A side character withholds a warning through a hand stop, eye widen, mouth almost speaking, small retreat, or forced smile. | Dialogue scenes, domestic conflict, public service encounters, institutional pressure. |

## Background Story Functions

Use these when the background is not necessarily horror but must tell story without dialogue.

| Function | What Background Carries | Common Carriers | Suspense Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Era / worldbuilding | Time period, social condition, rules of the world, city state, institution culture. | posters, screens, price tags, vehicles, uniforms, public notices, packaging, architecture, street behavior. | Ground the world fast without captions or voice-over. |
| Relationship / backstory | Divorce, grief, betrayal, cohabitation, parent-child absence, secret affair, old intimacy. | cropped photos, unmatched shoes, single table setting, children's object with dust, toothbrush count, wardrobe gaps, phone note names. | Give audience relational truth before characters admit it. |
| Time passage / state change | Months or years passed, neglect, recovery, decay, routine collapse. | plant growth or death, dust layers, calendar pages, faded tape marks, moved furniture, repeated photos, changing light season. | Replace "three months later" with visible physical aging. |
| Event / disaster prelude | Coming danger, outbreak, accident, social panic, system failure. | muted news, warning poster, public notice, temperature check, emergency drill, missing-person wall, closed routes. | Let danger arrive in background before plot speaks it. |
| Inner state externalized | Character depression, obsession, guilt, denial, mania, grief. | messy room zones, repeated notes, scratched photos, unopened bills, ritual arrangement, sleepless light, food untouched. | Make psychology visible without inner monologue. |
| Power / social rank | Who controls whom, hierarchy, humiliation, class distance, institutional pressure. | seating height, door access, guards, queue order, desk size, object ownership, who stands/who sits, who waits. | Build pressure before dialogue starts. |
| Irony / dark humor / contrast | What is said contradicts what the frame shows. | slogans, awards, propaganda, cheerful decor, ceremonial banners, public speech behind private failure. | Create cold satire and social suspense without explanation. |
| Foreshadowing / planted clue | Later truth shown early but treated as background texture. | newspaper headline, missing tool slot, old photo, half-covered warning, recurring number, object out of place. | Give second-watch reward and make plot feel inevitable. |

## Layered Information Rule

A background narrative shot can carry three layers, but each layer must be visually clean:

```text
surface layer: foreground action the viewer immediately understands
story layer: background detail that changes what the scene means
deep layer: social, psychological, moral, or structural implication
```

For AIGC short drama, keep one primary background information point and at most one supporting point per shot. More than that becomes visual noise.

## Camera Discipline

The camera should permit discovery, not point at the clue too loudly.

Use:

- locked-off frame with deep staging.
- slow neutral drift that follows foreground action, not the abnormality.
- foreground occlusion that lets background details peek through.
- reflection positioned inside an otherwise normal composition.
- focus held on the subject while background remains readable enough.
- wide or medium-wide frame when the background information matters.
- longer discovery window inside short-drama limits: 2-4 seconds for fixed shots, or a moving shot with a clear background beat.

Avoid turning the background clue into the obvious subject of the shot unless the story beat is "the character discovers it now".

## Sound Discipline

Background narrative often works because the sound lies.

Use:

- foreground foley masking the clue: typing, brushing teeth, eating, TV, kettle, hair dryer, running water, keyboard, traffic, elevator hum.
- normal room tone that continues unchanged while the background changes.
- slight absence instead of a loud cue: one appliance drops out, crowd tone thins, fluorescent buzz becomes more noticeable.
- NPC breath, swallowed word, halted cart wheel, or tiny hand contact when a side character notices.
- no musical announcement if the audience should discover the clue themselves.

## Timing For AIGC Short Drama

Compress the film mechanism:

- fixed shot discovery window: 2-4 seconds.
- background abnormality should begin after the foreground action is established.
- one shot contains one background information event.
- if the event needs more than 4 seconds, use a slow moving frame or split into two shots: setup frame -> payoff frame.
- end before the subject resolves the clue unless the next shot is the discovery reaction.

## Reusable Rules

- Everyday normality gives the background abnormality contrast.
- The clue should live in background, reflection, screen, side character, prop state, or space behavior.
- Audience discovery is the suspense engine; the camera should not over-explain.
- The subject should have visible reasons for not noticing: gaze down, phone, task, noise, body orientation, emotion, or occlusion.
- NPC reactions gain power through restraint: hand stop, step back, forced smile, eye line, unfinished word.
- Reflection is stronger than direct view because it makes the audience doubt what they saw.
- Background changes should stay unresolved long enough to make the audience want to warn the subject.
- The tail frame should preserve evidence: open door crack, changed object position, figure still in reflection, crowd still parted, elevator number lit.
- Natural placement is stronger than decorative placement. Background evidence should look like it belonged in the space before the camera arrived.
- One shot should carry one core information point. A second detail may support it; three or more details need a longer moving shot or another cut.
- Contrast creates narrative force: cheerful foreground with ruined background, official slogan with dirty reality, confident speech with background evidence of failure.
- Trust the audience. Let them infer the missing relationship, secret, or threat through objects, NPC behavior, and space instead of dialogue.
- Leave a gap. Background information should give enough to infer, not enough to flatten mystery.

## Failure Boundaries

- Do not turn the abnormality into a foreground reveal too early.
- Do not let the subject notice inside the same shot unless the shot is explicitly a discovery beat.
- Do not use a loud cue to explain where the audience should look.
- Do not make every extra react identically unless the scene is ritual/system pressure.
- Do not make the abnormality physically attack the subject in this lens; that becomes chase or attack staging.
- Do not explain the cause inside the shot; keep the frame as information gap.
- Do not make the background so subtle that the audience cannot discover it in a short-drama viewing window.

## Transferable Shot Templates

### Template A: Daily Foreground, Background Approach

```text
1. Establish normal action in foreground.
2. Keep subject gaze/task locked away from background.
3. Background figure/detail enters far layer slowly.
4. Foreground sound masks background motion.
5. Subject continues the normal task.
6. Tail frame leaves background closer or more legible, still undiscovered.
```

### Template B: Reflection Information Gap

```text
1. Compose subject with mirror/glass/screen inside the frame.
2. Let the subject look at something else or use the surface casually.
3. Reflection reveals an extra figure, missing object, wrong posture, or impossible spatial change.
4. Subject action interrupts their own chance to notice.
5. Tail frame keeps reflection abnormality visible or leaves a residual state.
```

### Template C: NPC Micro-Reaction Warning

```text
1. Subject moves through normal public or domestic action.
2. Side character sees something outside the subject's awareness.
3. Side character performs a restrained reaction: hand stop, eye widen, half-step back, mouth opens then closes.
4. Subject misreads the reaction or ignores it.
5. Tail frame holds the side character's fear while subject exits frame.
```

### Template D: Autonomous Object / Space Clue

```text
1. Subject performs a task with sound or visual attention elsewhere.
2. A background object changes state by itself.
3. The change has a physical trace: open gap, light on, liquid spill, shifted chair, number lit, curtain sway.
4. Foreground sound masks the event.
5. Tail frame holds the trace for audience recognition.
```

### Template E: Worldbuilding Through Environment

```text
1. Put the subject in a simple travel/action beat: walking, buying, entering, waiting, passing.
2. Fill background with era/world rules through natural carriers: signs, screens, clothes, vehicles, public behavior.
3. Let sound carry the setting: broadcast, vendor call, machinery, public notice, transport rhythm.
4. Keep the subject action simple so the audience reads the environment.
5. Tail frame leaves one memorable world-detail as the anchor.
```

### Template F: Relationship / Backstory Through Space

```text
1. Let the subject do a normal home or workplace action.
2. Arrange one core relationship evidence in background: cropped photo, empty seat, unused toothbrush, child's dusty object, missing clothing.
3. Add one supporting physical state: dust, fresh use, damage, folding, mismatch, wetness, unfinished task.
4. Keep character silent or emotionally restrained.
5. Tail frame holds the object relation for audience inference.
```

### Template G: Irony / Contrast Frame

```text
1. Foreground action or speech states one official truth.
2. Background evidence quietly contradicts it.
3. Keep both inside the same frame so the contradiction is visual, not verbal.
4. Sound remains realistic and ordinary.
5. Tail frame holds the slogan/object/action contrast after the subject exits or continues.
```

### Template H: Foreshadowing / Payoff Seed

```text
1. Place the future clue in a natural background location.
2. Let the foreground action draw first-view attention elsewhere.
3. Keep the clue readable but unannounced.
4. Later scene repeats or reframes the same object, number, mark, or phrase.
5. The audience realizes the earlier background already told them.
```

## Output Use

In formal storyboards, integrate this lens inside the normal row fields:

- `构图画面`: identify foreground story and background information carrier in the same drawable frame.
- `机位与运镜`: state how the camera permits discovery while staying loyal to foreground action.
- `画面内容`: record the subject action, background information event, NPC/prop/reflection reaction, and subject ignorance proof.
- `声音设计`: specify the masking foreground sound and the subtle background or NPC sound layer.
- `叙事目的`: name the information gap: what the audience knows before the subject.
- `SD承接锚点`: lock the background clue's location, readability, duration, and tail-frame evidence.

For broader narrative mode, name the background function in `叙事目的`: worldbuilding, relationship backstory, time passage, event prelude, inner-state externalization, power relation, irony, or foreshadowing.
