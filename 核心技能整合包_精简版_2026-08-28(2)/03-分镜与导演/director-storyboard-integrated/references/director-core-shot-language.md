# Director Core Shot Language

Use this file when a scene needs core film-language execution rather than surface style. It trains five reusable layers: gaze chain, blocking/axis/threshold, foreground occlusion, cut-point rhythm, and reaction-shot design.

## Use Order

Before writing a formal storyboard or a shot-scheme pool, run this compact pass:

1. `视线链`: who looks first, what the audience follows, who avoids eye contact, who owns the stare.
2. `站位/轴线/阈限`: who controls the center, who is trapped at a door/table/bed/window/elevator boundary, who crosses distance.
3. `前景遮挡`: what part of the truth is hidden by door crack, shoulder, curtain, glass, furniture, crowd, hair, rain, reflection.
4. `剪点`: which action is cut at the "almost" moment before it completes.
5. `反应镜头`: which human detail receives the event before the source is shown or instead of showing the source.

These layers are not decoration. They are the basic grammar that lets an image speak before dialogue explains.

## Module 1: 视线链 / 凝视语言

Core law: sightline is power and audience guidance. The first moving gaze in the frame tells the viewer where to look; the person who sustains a stare owns pressure; the person who looks away loses ground or hides something.

Reusable rules:

- One shot should carry at most three gaze links, such as `A notices object -> A checks B -> B avoids`.
- Power is held by the character who watches longer with fewer eye darts.
- Guilt, secrecy, and self-protection are readable through gaze avoidance before speech.
- Audience attention lands where a character looks first; use a character's eyes as an invisible pointer.
- A stare longer than seven seconds becomes pressure, desire, or judgment, so short-form versions compress it into eye hold, blink delay, and body stillness.

Failure boundaries:

- Too many gaze targets scatter the viewer.
- A character staring at nothing specific reads as empty acting.
- Explaining the same information in dialogue after a clear gaze chain weakens the image.
- Treating eye contact as generic romance or intimidation flattens power, guilt, discovery, and threat into one note.

Storyboard gate:

- `构图画面`: name the gaze path and the object/person each gaze lands on.
- `画面内容`: record eye path, blink, eye avoidance, head angle, hand pause, and the discovered object.
- `声音设计`: use pause, cutlery stop, room hum, breath catch, cloth rub, or a tiny object sound to make the gaze visible.
- `转场`: cut when the gaze lands, when the receiver senses it, or when the secret object leaves frame.

Transfer templates:

- Secret discovery: `viewer sees subject looking -> insert object edge -> subject looks at another person -> other person does not notice -> subject resumes ordinary action with one hand stalled`.
- Social pressure: `authority looks at one person -> group follows authority's gaze -> target realizes everyone is watching -> target lowers eyes or body shrinks`.
- Silent accusation: `one character holds stare -> accused glances down first -> object/hand/body confirms guilt`.

SD anchors:

- Write eye direction as a physical vector: `her eyes drop to the ticket corner in his pocket, then lift to his face`.
- Preserve gaze order and exact visible object.
- Use micro-expression anchors: eyelid hold, pupils widening, throat swallow, jaw set, fingers stopping mid-action.

## Module 2: 站位 / 轴线 / 阈限调度

Core law: position is relationship, space is power. Center, edge, distance, doorway, table, bed edge, elevator corner, and window line are story facts.

Reusable rules:

- The power center often sits or stands in the spatial center, at the table head, behind a desk, against a window, or blocking an exit.
- Emotional distance should be visible as physical distance. Closing distance escalates; retreating or leaning back yields ground.
- A threshold position means inner conflict: one foot inside and one foot outside, hand on doorknob, body stopped at bed edge, shoulder half in corridor light.
- A table, bed, doorway, counter, elevator door, or car door can become an axis line. Crossing it is a dramatic event.
- Blocking an exit is stronger than pointing a weapon, because the frame itself says there is no path.

Failure boundaries:

- Two people floating in an unmarked room lose relationship pressure.
- Dialogue scenes become flat when distance and body route stay unchanged.
- Crossing the axis without an emotional or informational reason confuses geography.
- A threshold shot loses force if the character is not visibly torn between two spaces.

Storyboard gate:

- `构图画面`: define foreground/midground/background, line of separation, distance, exit, and who owns the center.
- `机位与运镜`: protect screen direction, 180-degree axis, and the moment of crossing/retreat.
- `画面内容`: record foot placement, hand on boundary object, body lean, chair movement, retreat step, blocked exit.
- `叙事目的`: state what the position proves: dominance, hesitation, concealment, intimacy loss, social hierarchy, escape denial.

Transfer templates:

- Divorce/rupture: `one person inside warm room, one stuck at cold doorway, hand on knob, object between them, distance changes after one line`.
- Interrogation: `authority blocks door or sits behind desk; suspect retreats to wall/window; table remains the line no one crosses until escalation`.
- Family hierarchy: `table head stays empty or occupied; nearest/away seats map power; child or outsider sits closest to door`.

SD anchors:

- Name exact positions: left/right, inside/outside, near/far, one foot across threshold, hand gripping door handle.
- Keep axis stable across A segments: camera remains on the same side unless a visible crossing happens.
- Convert relationship into movement: chair scrape backward, elbow crossing table line, shoulder blocking doorframe.

## Module 3: 前景遮挡语言

Core law: partial information creates stronger suspense than full display. A foreground layer hides enough to make the viewer infer the missing part.

Reusable rules:

- Useful occlusion covers roughly 30%-70% of the frame or target; less becomes decoration, more becomes unreadable.
- Door cracks, shoulders, curtains, windshield rain, furniture edges, hair, crowd bodies, glass reflection, mirror edge, and darkness are information filters.
- The frame should reveal one meaningful fragment first: sleeve, shoe, eye, hand, key edge, breath fog, room light, object shadow.
- Occlusion works best when the viewer understands where the subject is but not what the subject fully does.
- Foreground can be calm while background contains danger, contradiction, or clue.

Failure boundaries:

- Full reveal too early drains viewer imagination.
- Occlusion with no readable fragment becomes visual mud.
- Repeating the same door-crack or shoulder block without new information becomes a trick.
- Hiding the wrong thing makes the scene confusing rather than tense.

Storyboard gate:

- `构图画面`: define the occluding layer, coverage percentage, visible fragment, and what remains outside the viewer's permission.
- `机位与运镜`: decide whether the camera peeks, slides, waits, or lets the foreground object pass.
- `画面内容`: record what fragment appears first and how it changes.
- `声音设计`: let sound carry the unseen area: cloth scrape, shoe shift, handle click, muffled voice, glass vibration.

Transfer templates:

- Door-crack reveal: `black door edge fills foreground -> narrow strip reveals one moving sleeve/eye/foot -> sound arrives before full body`.
- Crowd concealment: `passing body hides target -> target disappears behind shoulder layer -> one prop/hand remains visible`.
- Reflection occlusion: `foreground person stays normal -> reflection behind them carries the wrong movement`.

SD anchors:

- Specify the occluder as a stable physical layer: doorframe edge, shoulder silhouette, curtain fabric, rainy glass, mirror border.
- Name the visible fragment and its movement.
- Preserve foreground/midground/background separation so the model does not merge layers.

## Module 4: 剪点语言

Core law: cut at the almost moment. Suspense and rhythm sharpen when the cut lands before an action fully resolves: breath before scream, hand before turn, cup before impact, gaze before landing, door before full reveal.

Reusable rules:

- Cut on a breath peak, a hand touching an object, a foot about to cross, a cup leaving the table, a tear just forming, a door opening a slit, or a gaze starting to land.
- Sound can enter 1-2 frames early as a pre-lap, so the viewer feels the next space before seeing it.
- Short-drama rhythm should be fast, but each cut needs an information reason.
- Let rhythm follow body breath and object state, not arbitrary speed.
- After a key cut, the next image must answer, twist, or sharpen the previous expectation.

Failure boundaries:

- Cutting after the action fully finishes wastes suspense.
- Cutting too early before the viewer understands the setup makes the beat feel random.
- Fast cutting without object/body continuity becomes noise.
- Explaining the missing action later as dialogue weakens the cut.

Storyboard gate:

- `转场`: state the exact frame of the cut: before impact, before reveal, before turn, after sound pre-lap, after eye shift.
- `画面内容`: name the incomplete action that carries tension.
- `声音设计`: write J-cut/L-cut, impact withheld, silence drop, sound tail, room tone bridge.
- `叙事目的`: define what the viewer expects at the cut and how the next shot changes it.

Transfer templates:

- Door suspense: `hand touches doorknob -> metal click begins -> cut before knob turns -> next shot shows light strip or listener reaction`.
- Emotional reversal: `smile remains on face -> phone sound lands -> cut while smile has not faded -> next shot shows stunned eyes or frozen hand`.
- Object impact: `cup leaves hand -> cut before shatter -> next shot shows reaction, wet stain, or echo tail`.

SD anchors:

- Use time-coded action edges: `at the final half second her fingers touch the handle and stop`.
- Keep the continuity anchor in the A beat's `尾帧与连续性`: object position, hand contact, sound bridge, and landing frame. E indexes stay internal or in a separate production attachment.
- For a prompt segment, describe the cut as a landing state rather than abstract editing language when needed.

## Module 5: 反应镜头语言

Core law: humans empathize with the reaction more than the event source. Fear, grief, shock, and truth can be stronger when the source stays unseen and the body receives it.

Reusable rules:

- Reaction order: freeze -> body detail -> face.
- Strong reaction often lives in hands, throat, eyes, foot retreat, fingertips, breath, shoulders, object tremor, or a delayed blank stare.
- Extreme emotion may be blank, delayed, or too quiet; the visible delay can be more powerful than open crying or shouting.
- Keep 1-2 seconds of compressed stillness in short-form rhythm when the reaction is the reveal.
- The most powerful reaction may be no immediate reaction: body stays standing, hand stops, sound disappears.

Failure boundaries:

- Showing the source first can turn mystery into illustration.
- Big facial acting without body detail looks theatrical.
- A reaction with no trigger sound/object feels unmotivated.
- Cutting away before the body registers the event loses emotional transfer.

Storyboard gate:

- `画面内容`: start with what stops, then body detail, then face or lack of face.
- `构图画面`: choose body fragment, partial face, over-shoulder, profile, hands, throat, feet, or object in hand.
- `声音设计`: often use muffling, sudden room-tone drop, tinnitus, delayed impact, or tiny foley becoming huge.
- `转场`: land on the reaction before source reveal, or never reveal the source if the reaction carries enough story.

Transfer templates:

- Unseen threat: `sound from off-screen -> subject freezes -> hand tightens fabric/object -> throat swallow -> eyes widen toward unseen area`.
- Betrayal discovery: `phone/object glows -> hand stops scrolling -> screen dims -> subject stays seated and watches a doorway`.
- Family grief: `message arrives -> character continues normal task -> object handling fails -> delayed collapse into silence`.

SD anchors:

- Translate emotion into muscle and object action: pupils, eyelids, jaw, nostrils, lips, throat, shoulders, fingers, foot recoil, breath.
- Preserve trigger-to-reaction order.
- Use off-screen source as audible/visible pressure only when the frame itself withholds the source.

## Formal Storyboard Check

Before finalizing each row, answer:

- Whose gaze guides the frame?
- What position or boundary tells the relationship?
- What does the frame withhold, and through which foreground layer?
- Where is the cut placed relative to breath, hand, object, door, gaze, or sound?
- Which reaction detail carries the event?
- Which detail must survive into SD output?
